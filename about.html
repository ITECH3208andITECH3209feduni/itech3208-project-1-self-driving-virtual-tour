The Story

About the Kingston
Avenue of Honour

How a community came together in 1918 to plant 286 trees — one for each man and woman who served — creating a living memorial that endures more than a century later.

This project introduces the Avenue as the former Shire of Creswick Avenue of Honour at Kingston. From here, we use the name Kingston Avenue of Honour to help visitors find and recognise the Avenue. In August 1918, as the guns of the Western Front fell silent, the community gathered to plant something that would outlast the war — 250 elm trees, each bearing the name of a local man or woman who had served.

Further plantings brought the total to 286 trees. Nine commemorate women who served as nurses. Five remain unnamed. The trees stretch 2.9 kilometres along Kingston Road, starting at Victoria Road on the northern edge of the township and reaching toward the Midland Highway.

The Avenue of Honour encompasses communities across the former Shire: Kingston, Tourello, Yandoit Hills, Creswick, Smeaton, and surrounding areas — all bound together by sacrifice and remembrance.

Listed on the Victorian Heritage Register, this living monument is cared for by the Kingston Friends of the Avenue and the Creswick-Smeaton RSL.

🌳

Dutch Elm Trees

The Avenue's predominantly Dutch Elm trees were planted with enamel name plaques on wooden tree guards. Apart from replacing six trees, the Avenue remains intact.

🪨

The Monument

Harcourt granite, unveiled by Brigadier-General Elliot on 18 December 1927, marking the northern entrance to the Avenue.

👩‍⚕️

Nine Nurses

Nine trees commemorate women of the Australian Army Nursing Service. Their trees were adorned with red bows in 2014 by descendants.

📜

Heritage Listed

Listed on the Victorian Heritage Register in recognition of its significance to the community and Australia's wartime legacy.

🤝

Friends of the Avenue

The volunteer group, incorporated in 2014, cares for the trees and their history — organising tours, working bees, and collecting stories.
